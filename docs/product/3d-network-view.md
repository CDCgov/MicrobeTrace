# 3D Network

Open **View > 3D Network** to explore the same visible network in three dimensions. The pane can be docked beside 2D Network and other views.

On first opening, **Preparing 3D layout…** remains visible until the initial arrangement and camera framing are ready. The network then appears without a startup camera jump. **Freeze Layout** remains available to stop preparation early and show the current arrangement. Reopening a saved view restores its coordinates and camera directly; explicit recalculation continues to show the layout updating normally.

Use 3D as a complementary view for inspecting connections from different angles. Keep 2D Network available for an overview of larger networks.

When using many labels, shape frames, mixed-origin links, and arrows together, start with a filtered network around **250 nodes / 500 links**. Larger detailed networks can remain slow after freezing; reduce visual detail or filter further when needed. This starting point comes from a limited workstation benchmark; see the [capacity measurements](../performance/3d-network-capacity.md) for the tested settings and default-style guidance.

## Navigation and selection

- The toolbar's **Rotate / Pan** toggle chooses how dragging navigates the camera, using the same icon-and-text button style as Heatmap's **Zoom / Pan** control. **Rotate** is the default and uses Classic-style trackball navigation. Choose **Pan** to move the network with a left drag. Right-drag pans and scrolling zooms in either mode. The footer describes the active mode.
- Click a node to select it. Shift-, Ctrl-, or Command-click toggles additional nodes. Click the background or press Escape to clear selection.
- **Fit Network** frames the visible nodes. **Reset Camera** also restores the default viewing direction.
- The buttons surrounding the lower-right compass provide **XY** (above), **XZ** (left), **YZ** (right), and **Isometric** (below) camera orientations. XY puts X right and Y up, XZ puts X right and Z up, and YZ puts Y right and Z up. Isometric looks from positive X/Y/Z with Y upright. Presets keep the current pan target, camera distance, perspective settings, and node positions, and stop automatic rotation and camera inertia. Use **Fit Network** afterward if needed. Session saves and layout JSON exports retain the resulting camera pose; match the saved viewport size for reproducible framing.
- The lower-right **orientation compass** shows the world X (red), Y (green), and Z (blue) axes as you orbit or roll the camera, including during playback. Dashed arrows point away from you. An end-on axis appears as a dot toward you or a ring away from you.
- **Play camera rotation** turns the camera around its current target once every 30 seconds. Click **Pause camera rotation** to hold the current angle. Dragging the view, switching navigation modes, fitting/resetting the camera, recalculating the layout, or hiding/closing the pane stops playback. Rotation works with a frozen layout. Saved sessions retain the camera angle and reopen with playback paused.
- **Freeze Layout** holds node positions and stops force calculations while camera navigation remains available. **Resume Layout** restarts the forces. **Recalculate Layout** rebuilds the arrangement using deterministic starting positions.
- Focus the network and use `+` / `-` to zoom or `F` to fit. Separate zoom-in and zoom-out toolbar buttons are omitted, matching Heatmap's use of direct zoom gestures. Table and 2D Network remain available for accessible inspection of individual records.

## Settings and filtering

The settings dialog follows the 2D Network dialog's sizing, tabs, section names, and control layout. **Show Colors** keeps it open and places Global Settings alongside it. Node and link tooltips accept multiple fields. Nodes support labels, label size/orientation, borders, uniform size, and bounded **Size By** mapping. Links support labels and decimal formatting, uniform width or bounded **Width By** mapping (including reciprocal scaling), layout length, and arrows. Length is disabled while the layout is frozen. **Network > Display** controls neighbor highlighting.

Under **Nodes > Shapes and Sizes > Show Shapes**, choose a uniform shape or **Shape By** field. Available shapes are sphere, cube, cone, cylinder, diamond (octahedron), square pyramid, torus/ring, pill/capsule, and five-point star; assignments repeat beyond nine categories. Saved category assignments remain unchanged. The separate **3D Node Shapes** legend uses the shared table presentation, with shape previews, sorting, editable names/headings, count/frequency switches, collapse/expand, and **Show / Dock / Hide** modes. Counts and frequencies describe the visible graph. Assignments are saved per field and stay stable while filtering. The 2D shape table is identified as **2D Shapes**; editing either view's shapes leaves the other view unchanged.

**Show Shapes** respects the legend's saved docked or floating mode. A new legend, or reopening a hidden legend, follows the other shape/key tables' display mode, defaulting to docked. **Border Width** controls the thickness in screen pixels of the silhouette and structural edges. Smooth spheres, toruses, and pills have no internal grid; cones/cylinders retain their cap rims; faceted solids retain actual face boundaries without triangulation diagonals. Set it to zero to hide outlines. Outlines follow camera rotation, sizing, filtering, and export, and retain category transparency and neighbor dimming.

Shapes mapped to the same size use approximately equal average projected area, improving comparable visual prominence across families. Individual angles still differ; this is neither equal volume nor a validated perceptual mapping. The nonconvex torus/star multipliers account for holes and gaps. **Size By** maps numeric values between Min Size and Max Size, rather than allowing outliers to grow without bounds. Size settings keep their saved values, although the refined nonspherical meshes occupy more space than the old equal-radius shapes. Labels and arrow clearance use the actual shape bounds. Geometries, label textures, outline buffers, and materials are shared and released when no longer needed or when the view closes. Colors, per-category transparency, and background follow global settings; selected nodes use the shared selection color. Moving the link **Transparency** slider right makes links more transparent, while saved `3DNet-link-transparency` values retain their old meaning so older sessions keep their appearance. **Use legend transparency** removes the local link-opacity override. Threshold, nearest-neighbor, cluster, subset, and timeline filters use the existing shared network rules. Only links with two visible endpoints are rendered.

The renderer's [Balanced geometry](../../src/app/visualizationComponents/ThreeDComponent/three-d-node-geometry.ts) uses a 20 × 12 sphere, 24-sided cones/cylinders, minimal faceted solids, a 28 × 10 torus tube, a 20-sided capsule, and a 36-triangle extruded star. Matte Lambert surfaces, a stronger oblique key light with restrained ambient/fill, and subtle face tones make shape boundaries clearer while keeping category colors. The optimized diamond is taller, the torus has a larger opening, the capsule is longer, and the star is deeper; projected-area factors were recalibrated. Faceted shapes retain structural edges without a duplicate silhouette pass. Opaque surfaces/silhouettes are instanced and structural edges are combined by shape/detail/width; transparent and neighbor-dimmed nodes keep individual meshes. Invisible per-node geometry retains selection and hover behavior. Legend and picker thumbnails are cached images captured from the same app geometry and lighting, without live graphics contexts in the tables.

**Nodes > Shapes and Sizes** also offers **Shape Style** (Optimized / Earlier refined), **Detail** (Balanced / Adaptive to zoom / Fine), and **Lighting** (Fixed / Camera fill). Balanced and Fixed remain defaults. Adaptive uses lower detail below about 17 px and higher detail above about 85 px, with hysteresis to avoid switching repeatedly around a boundary; it changes tessellation without changing node values or coordinates. Image snapshots use Balanced for Adaptive, or Fine when explicitly chosen. Camera fill follows the viewing direction using the existing fill light. These independent `3DNet-*` choices are saved in sessions and exported layout JSON. Earlier refined preserves the previous refinement's proportions/outlines; older files without a style key use Optimized by default. A torus loses its hole edge-on, a pill looks round end-on, and a star is less distinctive edge-on; orbit or use XY to inspect the torus/star face.

The [direct original-versus-latest benchmark](../performance/3d-shape-original-vs-latest-2026-11-17.md) measures the complete shape update against the six coarse shapes we started with. The [intermediate optimization benchmark](../performance/3d-shape-optimization-2026-11-17.md) covers the later refinement step separately. The three added shape families have no original counterparts. Geometric normalization is not a completed human perception study. Standalone review tools, screenshots, and email drafts are maintained outside the application repository.

When **Color Links By** is **Origin**, dual-origin links show the first origin as a solid stroke and the second as dashed bands, matching the two origins in the Duo-Link legend and the 2D view. Each origin keeps its own color and legend transparency; the local link Transparency override applies to both. The bands follow the link in 3D during layout and camera navigation, support mapped widths and neighbor highlighting, and appear in exported images. Choosing another color field restores ordinary solid links.

Distance labels and tooltips follow the global metric and TN93 display format: percentages for TN93 percentage display and whole numbers for SNP counts. Label precision remains independent of 2D. Distance labels are omitted when the distance is unavailable or its origin is filtered out; zero TN93 labels are suppressed, while zero SNP counts remain visible.

**Links > Shapes and Sizes > Arrows** reveals arrowheads only for records marked as directed. **Bidirectional** adds a reverse arrowhead only for directed records also marked as bidirectional. Both switches are independent of the 2D settings and are saved as `3DNet-link-directed` and `3DNet-link-bidirectional`. Arrowheads have a contrasting outline and scale with link thickness so they extend beyond opaque links. They follow layout changes and neighbor highlighting and appear in image exports; they do not add links or forces to the graph.

Grouping surfaces, collapsed aggregate nodes, geographic/cluster layouts, and mixed-color node rings remain 2D features. The 3D view continues to use solid node colors and a force-directed layout.

The Classic `3DNet-*` widget names are retained for imported styles and sessions. Legacy layout names `3d_network` and `3dnetwork` restore the new pane. Older sessions without 3D camera state receive a new layout.

## Saving and images

Session files and local session links store a versioned `session.meta.threeDViewState` record with camera position, camera target, camera up direction (roll), node positions by ID, and the frozen-layout setting. Older saved camera states use the upright default. These positions are independent of shared node coordinates and the 2D renderer. Positions survive filtering and pane closure. Saved positions are restored without rearranging the network; topology changes can resume layout when it is not frozen. **Reset Camera** restores an upright view.

Fresh layouts and **Recalculate Layout** use stable node/link ordering, deterministic 3D phyllotaxis starting positions, locally seeded force jitter, and a fixed budget of 150 simulation steps. The layout no longer stops early because a wall-clock timer expired. With the same visible graph, link length, application version, and an uninterrupted calculation, recalculation reproduces the same coordinates. Filtering, freezing early, and resuming an existing layout can change its history; use a coordinate snapshot to preserve a particular arrangement. Large layouts may take longer to settle; the performance advisory and Freeze Layout remain available.

In **Export Network Image > Layout and camera**, choose **Export layout and camera (JSON)** to save the visible nodes' ID-keyed x/y/z coordinates, visible link endpoints, camera position/target/up and perspective settings, original view dimensions, application version, and layout algorithm/force seed/iteration limit/link length. New files also include shape style, detail policy, lighting mode, size-policy version, and fixed image-export detail. The snapshot records the current arrangement even if captured before the layout settles. This export does not change the live layout or camera playback.

To reuse the snapshot, load the same network, apply the same filters, and open **3D Network Settings > Network > Layout > Restore layout and camera**. The visible node IDs and links must match; invalid or mismatched files leave the current view unchanged. Restoration freezes the saved positions and pauses camera playback. Match the exported viewport dimensions for the same figure framing. The JSON contains layout information rather than source attributes or color settings; save a full MicrobeTrace session alongside it to preserve data, filters, styling, and legends. Older sessions without perspective settings continue to load normally.

**Export Network Image** follows the 2D export dialog: filename and file type share a row; **Advanced** contains Scale (0.1–2), a live network-resolution readout, and Quality. Quality affects JPEG/WebP; PNG remains lossless. Resolution describes the network image before key tables and padding are added. Displayed node/link color keys and the independent 3D shape legend (including previews and display names) are included automatically; hidden keys and the 2D shape legend are excluded. The scope label identifies the full or filtered visible network. Cancel closes without downloading, and Export closes the dialog and shows shared progress while the image is prepared. The 3D-only layout download remains under **Layout and camera**. Dashboard export preserves the 3D WebGL frame. Image scaling changes output dimensions; it does not add geometric detail beyond the rendered canvas.

## Implementation and lifecycle

`ThreeDComponent` integrates with the shared view lifecycle. `ThreeDNetworkRenderer` owns the lazy-loaded, pinned `3d-force-graph`/Three.js renderer, copied graph records, physics coordinates, and graphics resources. Tooltips are created as text nodes. Rapid events are coalesced into one update per animation frame. Hidden panes and background documents pause animation and simulation; showing a pane reconciles shared state. Closing it removes listeners, disposes the renderer, and releases its WebGL context. WebGL failures offer Retry and Open 2D Network.

3D is registered as its own component and does not inherit from or call `TwoDComponent`. Its appearance controls write only `3DNet-*` settings; 2D node labels, tooltips, sizing, and link appearance retain their existing keys. Its camera and coordinates live in `threeDViewState`, separately from 2D's `rendererViewState`. Shared data, colors, filtering, and selection remain intentional application-wide behavior. Unit tests check both directions of appearance-setting isolation.

Node dragging/pinning, imported custom shape models, persistent labels, cluster surfaces, and video export are follow-up work. The default view renders all visible links. See the measured graph-size recommendations below.

### Recommended graph sizes

A **Large network in 3D** advisory appears before rendering a graph with more than **750 visible nodes or 1,500 visible links**. It recommends filtering through **Settings > Filtering**, or letting the layout settle and choosing **Freeze Layout** for smoother camera navigation. Users can continue in 3D or open 2D Network. Graphs above **2,000 nodes or 4,000 links** receive stronger guidance to filter or use 2D because freezing alone may not provide smooth navigation. Counts describe the filtered graph; these thresholds are advisory guidelines, not enforced limits. Within an open 3D pane, routine updates do not repeat an acknowledged advisory unless the graph reaches the higher tier or a different dataset is loaded.

The [October 29 capacity benchmark](../performance/3d-network-capacity.md) replaces the preliminary pre-trackball measurements with five runs of eleven graph sizes. On the tested Chrome / Intel Arc setup, start with about **750 visible nodes and 1,500 visible links** for smooth navigation while layout is running. **1,000 / 2,000** is an upper everyday tier with less consistent layout responsiveness. After layout stops, freezing permits smooth inspection at **2,000 / 4,000**. At **5,000 / 10,000**, rotation averaged about 25 fps frozen and 5 fps during layout, so that size is not recommended for fluid navigation with the current renderer.

These are recommendations for the measured hardware, viewport, graph structures, and default styling, not hard limits. Use shared subset filters to inspect a smaller visible graph. No nodes or links are silently sampled away. The linked report includes the measurements, limitations, and opt-in Cypress benchmark commands.

The implementation was also checked in Chrome for real canvas selection, camera navigation, settings, subset and timeline filtering, pane/session restoration, Classic session launch and settings, selection synchronization with both 2D renderers, PNG and dashboard capture, and recovery from context loss. Closing a pane released its graphics context. The production build and 21 focused model/Sigma adapter unit tests passed.
